How to read the numbers

Every number carries its source. paper and repo are printed sources. slide is a projected slide. talk† is a number spoken in an auto-captioned talk: treat it as approximate, as reported by the speaker. measured means it was run on a 4-core CPU in a cloud container while building this manual. Cosmos claims name their generation (1, 2.5 or 3), because the models changed a lot in twenty months.

Part I · Foundations

1What Physical AI is

Physical AI is AI that perceives and acts in the physical world: robot arms, humanoids, mobile robots, cars, factories and cities. Language models learn from text the internet already holds. Physical AI has no such corpus. The data it needs (contact forces, friction, motor noise, rare accidents) mostly does not exist yet, and collecting it with real machines is slow, costly and sometimes dangerous.

The field's answer is to compute the data. Simulators generate trajectories with exact ground truth. Generative world models turn those simulations into photoreal video and invent rare scenarios. A small amount of real data anchors the whole thing. The rest of this manual is about the tools for each step and how they fit.

Three computers

Every speaker in the source material uses the same frame. A system needs three computers, and each one constrains the others.

The data pyramid

TierExampleStrengthLimit
Real robot data (top)Teleoperation with a headset mapping your hands to robot handsHighest fidelity, real motor noiseScales with robots and people. One operator-hour yields at most one hour of data; resets often leave only minutes of useful data talk†

The scale gap is the motivation for everything else. In the GR00T N1 paper, Table 7 lists 3,288.8 hours of real-robot data, 2,517.0 hours of human video, 1,742.6 hours of simulation, and 827.3 hours of neural trajectories, for 8,375.7 hours total. The neural set expands 88.4 hours of in-house teleoperation to 827.3 hours. Real demonstrations still seed the loop. Compute is how the field scales everything past that seed.

2The stack, layer by layer

Each layer reads and writes the one below it. Learn them bottom-up: the data model first, because every tool above speaks it.

Two relationships are easy to get wrong. Newton does not replace PhysX. Newton's own FAQ answers "Will Newton replace PhysX? No." repo: PhysX stays the engine for industrial digital twins, Newton targets robot learning. And Cosmos does not replace simulation. A research lead put the division of labor this way: "Omniverse provides 3D consistency and Cosmos provides photorealism and imagination" talk†.

The AV community describes simulation in three generations. They are complementary, not successive: each covers a weakness of the others.

The sim-to-real data flywheel

Every talk in the source material describes some version of this loop. The order matters, so it is numbered.

  1. Seed with a little real data: tens of teleoperation demos, fleet drives, archived video.
  2. Reconstruct or compose worlds: NuRec turns logs into editable Gaussian scenes; Isaac Sim and SimReady assets compose new ones.
  3. Multiply trajectories in physics: DexMimicGen generated about 21,000 demonstrations from 60 human source demos (arXiv 2410.24185); on a real humanoid can-sorting task its generated data produced a 90% success policy, against 0% from the human demos alone. Isaac Lab runs thousands of environments.
  4. Multiply appearance with Cosmos Transfer: segmentation, depth and edges from the simulator condition photoreal video, so labels survive.
  5. Dream new behaviors: post-train a world model on the robot's own data, prompt new tasks, recover actions with inverse dynamics (GR00T Dreams).
  6. Filter generated data with a reasoning model that grades physical plausibility.
  7. Curate and search for gaps and corner cases.
  8. Train on real plus synthetic. In GR00T N1, neural augmentation expands in-house teleop from 88.4 h to 827.3 h. Real-robot hours in Table 7 are 3,288.8 h, larger than the simulation-plus-neural portion.
  9. Evaluate somewhere else: another simulator, a reconstructed scene, the real robot.
  10. Deploy and feed back: failures become the next seed.
The experiment to remember

X-Mobility (arXiv 2410.17491), an NVIDIA navigation model trained on Isaac Sim synthetic data, transferred zero-shot to a real Nova Carter. The sim-to-sim test in chapter 8 shows the same discipline on a CPU. Evaluate somewhere other than where you generated: a policy that only works in its training simulator has proven only that it can exploit that simulator.

Part II · Worlds and physics

4OpenUSD and SimReady

OpenUSD (Universal Scene Description) is the scene format every tool in the stack reads and writes. For a software engineer, the right mental model is a version-control system for scenes. A layer is a file of opinions about prims (scene objects) and their properties. A stage is the composed result of many layers. Nothing is overwritten; stronger opinions win.

Composition: who wins

Conflicts resolve by the strength order LIVERPS, strongest first: Local, Inherits, VariantSets, Relocates, References, Payload, Specializes. Through OpenUSD v24.11 the glossary was LIVRPS, before Relocates. In practice:

  • def defines a prim, over patches a prim defined elsewhere, class is an abstract base for inherits.
  • A reference brings in another file's prim. A payload is a lazy reference you can leave unloaded: the tool for heavy geometry and optional features.
  • A variant set switches between alternatives (color, gripper type, level of detail) inside one asset.
  • Anything ending in API is an applied schema that adds behavior to a prim: PhysicsRigidBodyAPI, MassAPI, MaterialBindingAPI.
Gotcha

Adding a referenced asset as a sublayer and editing it edits the original file. Sublayers share authority; references do not.

SimReady: assets a robot can use

Graphics assets are hollow shells: no joints, no colliders, no mass. SimReady is the content specification that fixes that: validated articulations and joint limits, efficient collision proxies (convex hulls for speed, signed distance fields for tight tolerances), solver-friendly meshes, correct units and pivots, semantic labels, and materials that survive generative re-rendering (glass and printed text are the known failure cases) talk†.

Isaac Sim 6 documents a layered USD asset. Typical files are base.usda, geometries.usd, instances.usda, materials.usda, and physics.usd, composed into one entry, asset.usd or interface.usda. Feature opinions (physics, sensors, ROS, control) stay in their own layers and load as payloads.

Authoring the physics feature layer with the pxr Python API

from pxr import Usd, UsdGeom, UsdPhysics

stage = Usd.Stage.CreateNew("asset_physics.usda")
UsdGeom.SetStageUpAxis(stage, UsdGeom.Tokens.z)      # robotics is Z-up
UsdGeom.SetStageMetersPerUnit(stage, 1.0)

root = stage.OverridePrim("/quadruped")              # an "over": opinions on prims defined in other layers
stage.SetDefaultPrim(root)
UsdPhysics.ArticulationRootAPI.Apply(root)           # exactly one articulation root, on the top robot prim

base = stage.OverridePrim("/quadruped/base")
UsdPhysics.RigidBodyAPI.Apply(base)
UsdPhysics.MassAPI.Apply(base).CreateMassAttr(10.0)  # kilograms
stage.Save()
Measured while building this manual

A 10-layer quadruped asset built this way passed a 13-check validator (default prim, Z-up, units, one articulation root, joint limits, mass and collider on every body, payloads resolve) and loaded into Newton with the same 13 bodies, 18 degrees of freedom and 30.702 kg as the URDF. After 1 s of simulation the base sat at 0.4585 m from USD versus 0.4587 m from URDF measured.

The standard is still growing. An AOUSD proposal adds deformable bodies (volumes, surfaces, curves, attachments) to UsdPhysics. Newton 1.4.0 (16 July 2026) is the first Newton release that adds an experimental importer for that schema repo.

5Omniverse: the platform

Omniverse began as a digital-twin platform. Isaac was built on it, and Physical AI adopted it. Its job is to compose OpenUSD scenes in real time, render them with physically based sensors, and stream them.

Kit and extensions

Apps are built from the Kit App Template. NVIDIA deprecated the Omniverse Launcher on 1 October 2025. Functionality lives in extensions: an extension.toml manifest plus Python that implements omni.ext.IExt and an omni.ui window.

Sensors

Physically based cameras with lens models, LiDAR, and radar from the same scene, each with ground truth.

Replicator and Nucleus

Replicator randomizes scenes and writes labeled data (chapter 9). Nucleus stores shared assets and live sessions.

Gotcha

Add extensions to the app's .kit file before building the app; adding them afterwards does not register them talk†.

6Physics engines and Newton

Game physics optimizes for speed and plausibility. Robot learning needs fidelity and robustness, because small modeling errors "quickly turn into unrealistic behaviors" in a learned policy talk†. It also needs engines that researchers can modify, that run thousands of worlds in parallel, and that speak tensors and gradients.

Newton is the answer from NVIDIA, Google DeepMind and Disney Research: an open-source GPU physics engine built on NVIDIA Warp, with MuJoCo Warp as its main rigid-body backend, governed as a Linux Foundation project repo. Newton 1.0 reached general availability on 16 March 2026 at GTC 2026, after a beta unveiled in September 2025, and Isaac Lab 3.0 integrates it natively. v1.4.0 followed on 16 July 2026; check the releases page for later versions repo.

Five classes and one loop

The core of Newton's double-pendulum example (abridged from newton/examples/basic/example_basic_pendulum.py)

builder = newton.ModelBuilder()
link_0 = builder.add_link()
builder.add_shape_box(link_0, hx=1.0, hy=0.1, hz=0.1)
j0 = builder.add_joint_revolute(parent=-1, child=link_0, axis=wp.vec3(0.0, 1.0, 0.0), ...)
builder.add_articulation([j0, ...], label="pendulum")
builder.add_ground_plane()

model = builder.finalize()
solver = newton.solvers.SolverXPBD(model)
state_0, state_1 = model.state(), model.state()
control = model.control()
newton.eval_fk(model, model.joint_q, model.joint_qd, state_0)   # not required for MuJoCo; required for others

pipeline = newton.CollisionPipeline(model)
contacts = pipeline.contacts()

for _ in range(sim_substeps):                 # 10 substeps per 1/100 s frame in the example
    state_0.clear_forces()
    pipeline.collide(state_0, contacts)
    solver.step(state_0, state_1, control, contacts, sim_dt)
    state_0, state_1 = state_1, state_0       # swap states

On a GPU the substep loop is recorded once with wp.ScopedCapture() and replayed as a CUDA graph, which removes Python and launch overhead from every frame. Many worlds come from builder.replicate(template, world_count=N); world -1 holds shared entities such as the ground plane.

Contact and deformables

The collision pipeline runs a broad phase (explicit pairs, n×n, or sweep-and-prune), then a narrow phase: MPR and GJK for convex shapes, BVH for raw meshes, precomputed signed distance fields for constant-time mesh queries, and opt-in hydroelastic contact that computes contact over an area instead of at points. Area contact maps naturally onto tactile sensing. A deterministic mode gives bit-exact repeatability on the same GPU architecture, as long as the solver is deterministic too repo.

Deformables come in four dimensions: particles and MPM (0D), Cosserat rods with hysteresis so cables keep their bent shape (1D), thin shells with continuous collision (2D), and tetrahedral soft bodies (3D). Divide and Truncate (Chen et al., arXiv 2604.15513; SIGGRAPH 2026) dropped 200 layers of cloth (4M vertices, 7.84M triangles) onto a cylinder, sustaining more than 20 million simultaneous contact pairs without penetration. ImplicitMPM’s documentation follows the 2016 Daviet and Bertails-Descoubes method; Daviet’s SIGGRAPH 2026 paper, Mixed Material Point Methods for Stiff Elastoplasticity, extends that line of work to stiff elastoplastic materials.

Why GPUs matter here

Isaac Lab runs thousands of environments in parallel on a GPU, with PhysX or Newton underneath. The task id Isaac-Velocity-Rough-G1-v0 is in the Isaac Lab repository.

Conventions that bite
  • Z is up, and quaternions are XYZW. MuJoCo and Isaac use WXYZ.
  • Maximal-coordinate solvers (XPBD, SemiImplicit, VBD) need newton.eval_fk after finalize().
  • Call SolverX.register_custom_attributes(builder) before adding entities that need them.
  • macOS runs CPU-only. APIs move monthly, so pin the version.

A solver bake-off you can reproduce

To see how much solver choice matters, the same quadruped was dropped from 0.7 m and held in a crouch by joint drives, with six solver paths, 100 frames per second and 10 substeps (1 ms per step), on a 4-core CPU measured.

XPBD and MuJoCo Warp trajectories were bit-identical across runs. And Newton's sand-walking example mpm_anymal couples one way: the robot pushes the sand, but the sand never pushes back, so the robot's path on sand is identical to its path on rigid ground. For a terrain study you need two-way coupling.

7Isaac Sim and ROS 2: software in the loop

Isaac Sim is NVIDIA's robot simulator on Omniverse: URDF and MJCF import, physical sensors, OmniGraph visual scripting, and a ROS 2 bridge. Its main use is software in the loop: run the robot's real software stack (Nav2, MoveIt 2, perception) against a virtual body before touching hardware. Isaac Sim is the body and senses; a ROS 2 container is the brain; both share one DDS domain.

The drive graph, node by node

A differential-drive robot turns Nav2's velocity command into wheel speeds through an Action Graph. This is the graph from a live OmniGraph inspector for a Nova Carter robot, with its real values:

  1. OnPlaybackTick fires every frame and provides deltaSeconds.
  2. ROS2Context joins DDS domain 0 (or reads ROS_DOMAIN_ID).
  3. ROS2SubscribeTwist listens on cmd_vel with a queue of 10.
  4. Two BreakVector3 nodes pull out linear.x and angular.z.
  5. DifferentialController on a Nova Carter: wheel radius 0.14 m and wheel distance 0.413 m, the values in NVIDIA's Nova Carter ROS graph tutorial and in Isaac Sim's MobilityGen Carter config. The wheel joints are joint_wheel_left and joint_wheel_right. A wrong wheel distance scales every commanded yaw rate by the same error.
  6. IsaacArticulationController drives the two wheel joints, named through a ConstructArray of tokens.

Further graphs publish what a real robot would: /scan or a point cloud from LiDAR, rgb and camera_info from a render product, IMU readings, odometry, the TF tree, and /clock so every node runs on simulation time.

Bringing up Nav2

Order matters. Navigation does nothing until the transform chain map → odom → base_link exists.

  1. Map server loads the occupancy map.
  2. AMCL localizes and publishes map → odom.
  3. Planner server computes global paths.
  4. Controller server follows them and publishes cmd_vel.
  5. Behavior tree navigator ties goals to planner and controller.

Arms: MoveIt 2

For a Franka Panda, Isaac Sim publishes joint states on isaac_joint_states and subscribes to commands on isaac_joint_commands. MoveIt's ros2_control description is switched to a topic-based hardware interface that talks to those two topics, and its controller file needs the action_ns and default keys the Setup Assistant leaves out talk†.

Gotchas from the tutorials
  • Put the articulation root on the top-level robot prim, or you get "no robot prim found".
  • Wheels use velocity drives (stiffness 0, high damping); steering knuckles use position drives.
  • Containers on one host must share ROS_DOMAIN_ID; the Isaac side should use UDP-only DDS, because shared memory cannot cross the container boundary.
  • Stamp messages with simulation time, or TF lookups fail in confusing ways.

8Isaac Lab and robot learning

Isaac Lab is the GPU-parallel reinforcement- and imitation-learning framework on Isaac Sim, with PhysX or Newton underneath.

Anatomy of a task

A manager-based task is a set of configuration classes:

PartWhat it definesExample
SceneRobot, objects, terrain, sensors, replicated across environmentsG1 humanoid and a box on a table
ActionsWhat the policy outputsJoint position targets for the arm
ObservationsWhat the policy seesJoint positions and velocities, object pose, last action
CommandsGoals sampled per episodeTarget velocity or target position
RewardsWeighted terms summed each stepDense "wrist closer to object", "object closer to target", a success bonus
TerminationsWhen an episode endsTime-out, object dropped, robot fell
EventsDomain randomizationMass, friction, pushes, sensor noise
 
python scripts/reinforcement_learning/rsl_rl/train.py --task Isaac-Velocity-Rough-G1-v0 --num_envs 10000 --headless --max_iterations 500
python scripts/reinforcement_learning/rsl_rl/play.py  --task Isaac-Velocity-Rough-G1-Play-v0 --checkpoint logs/rsl_rl/.../model_499.pt
Gotchas
  • Running play.py on a custom task without --checkpoint loads an untrained policy, and the robot does not move.
  • Ending an episode on success can teach the policy to avoid the goal if success cuts off future reward. Paying the bonus every step at the goal avoids that.
  • --num_envs trades throughput against GPU memory.

Sim-to-sim before sim-to-real

A policy that works in the simulator it was trained in has only proven it can exploit that simulator. The humanoid teams use another physics engine as a litmus test before hardware talk†. Export the policy to ONNX, map the joint order between engines (Isaac Lab and MuJoCo order joints differently), rebuild the observation vector, and run it elsewhere.

Measured while building this manual

The PhysX-trained ANYmal C walking policy that ships with Newton ran in Newton on a CPU and walked 2.714 m in 3.0 s without falling. The joint-order maps derived from joint names matched the arrays hard-coded in Newton's own example measured.

Part III · Data from pixels

9Synthetic data for perception

Perception models need labeled images at a scale no annotation team can produce. A simulator knows the exact class, box, depth and segmentation of every pixel, so labels are free. The work is making the images varied enough that a model trained on them works on real cameras.

Domain randomization with Replicator

  1. Load a scene (a warehouse) and the target asset (a pallet jack).
  2. Register randomizers: camera pose, target color and pose, floor and wall textures, lighting, distractor objects.
  3. Attach a writer (KITTI or COCO) and generate frames; the tutorial used 2,000 per distractor set.
  4. Hold out a validation split (14% in the tutorial) and fine-tune a detector.

Replicator’s job is the method: randomize the scene, write KITTI or COCO labels, and fine-tune a detector on the result.

From synthetic-looking to photoreal

Renders still look rendered. Cosmos Transfer takes the simulator's depth, segmentation or edge video plus a text prompt and produces photoreal video with the same geometry, so the labels still line up. In the Cosmos Predict 2.5 paper, Transfer augmentation took a real-robot Diffusion Policy trained on 100 demos from 1/30 to 24/30 successes, against 5/30 with classic image augmentation paper.

Conditioning is control

What you feed the world model is preserved; what you leave out, it is free to imagine. To vary shadows, condition on segmentation, which has no baked shadows. To keep lane markings, include them in the condition. For a pedestrian that only needs to be "a person in a box", an HD map plus bounding boxes is enough talk†.

Part III · Data from pixels

10Neural reconstruction: real places as simulations

3D Gaussian splatting represents a scene as millions of small 3D Gaussians, each with a position, a covariance (its size and orientation), an opacity and a view-dependent color. They are fitted to camera images by differentiable rasterization and rendered in real time. NuRec is NVIDIA's productized pipeline for doing this from vehicle and robot sensor logs, rendering cameras and LiDAR from one representation.

The pipeline, as Mcity ran it

  1. Capture with a calibrated camera and lidar rig, plus poses. Mcity’s public description uses roof cameras, GPS/IMU, and LiDAR with time sync.
  2. Convert the ROS bag to the reconstruction input format and generate auxiliary data: segmentation and an ego-vehicle mask.
  3. Preview with Instant NuRec, a feed-forward model: about 1.5 s for a 10–20 s multi-camera clip, against about 75 min of per-scene optimization for full NuRec; adding LiDAR takes it to about 20 s (arXiv 2607.14203).
  4. Run the full reconstruction on an RTX PRO 6000 or H100.
  5. Export a USDZ scene and serve it to simulators (Isaac Sim, CARLA, AlpaSim).
Mcity, a 32-acre test environmentWhat is public
Facility size32 acres (University of Michigan, 20 July 2015)
2026 member-meeting talkAbout a year and a couple hundred thousand dollars the older way, versus about a week of GPU time with NuRec

The weakness: leaving the recorded path

A reconstruction is only well constrained where the cameras looked. Drive a different line and you see surfaces from angles nobody recorded. NuRec’s helpers address this: Fixer repairs off-trajectory artifacts, Asset Harvester lifts objects out for reuse, and Harmonizer relights inserted objects. The OmniDreams paper (arXiv 2606.03159) measures the effect: as trajectory deviation grows, NuRec’s FVD rises quickly while the generative model stays much more stable.

How Cosmos 3 works

The generator is trained with rectified flow, the same velocity-regression family as Predict 2.5, with a masked mean-squared error and a separate noise level per modality. On Cosmos3-Super, Physics-IQ image-to-video is 43.8, rising to 48.9 with WMReward best-of-N. Video-to-video is 59.7, rising to 63.4. Cosmos-Surg-dVRK’s pooled correlation with the real robot is Pearson r = 0.718 (p < 0.001). The average of the four per-task correlations is 0.707.

The key idea is that action is a modality: "a modality whose tokens represent transitions between consecutive visual states" repo. Each ego or effector pose is 9D (3D translation and 6D rotation, mapped back to a rotation matrix by SVD). Figure 3 totals: an autonomous vehicle is 9D, a single arm is 10D, a humanoid is 29D, and an egocentric human is 57D. A simple gripper adds one dimension. A five-finger hand can be 15D.

The Cosmos 3 report (Table 19) gives Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID 39.7% on RoboLab-120 with specific instructions and 36.8% with default instructions. On the public RoboLab-120 overall leaderboard: Cosmos3-Nano-Policy 36.8%, π0.5 28.0%, GR00T N1.6 7.2%. In September 2026, Black Forest Labs' 7B FLUX 3 Action reported 42.92%, above Cosmos 3 Nano. The report’s RoboArena first place is the ranking at 14:40 on 30 May 2026.

Limits, in Cosmos 3's own words

The README warns of artifacts in long, high-resolution or physically complex outputs, temporal inconsistency, object morphing, inaccurate 3D structure and implausible dynamics, and says that "applications that require physically grounded simulation, safety-critical control, or complex multi-agent behavior need additional validation" repo. A world model preserves what you condition on and imagines the rest. It is not a physics engine.

Closing the loop: Cosmos-Dreams

For AV testing, NVIDIA showed a real-time, action-conditioned world model in a closed loop talk†:

OmniDreams (arXiv 2606.03159) and the FlashDreams runtime are NVIDIA’s published 2026 line of action-conditioned driving world models, with results on a NuRec autonomous-vehicle dataset. A cheaper simulator is useful when it ranks policies the way a trusted one does.

Measured on a toy version

A CPU toy of this loop (a kinematic car, a rasterized "renderer" of the abstract state, five policies, four renderers of increasing degradation, 10 seeds) checks whether each renderer ranks the policies like the exact one. Kendall tau fell from 1.0 (mild) to 0.6 (moderate) to 0.2 (severe) measured. The method generalizes: a cheaper simulator is useful if it ranks policies the way a trusted one does.

Part IV · Applications

12Robot brains and humanoids

Why humanoids? NVIDIA's GEAR lab gives four reasons: versatility, falling cost, a world already built for human bodies, and data, because human video and motion capture transfer to a human-shaped robot talk†.

GR00T N1: two systems

GR00T N1 (18 March 2025) borrows the fast/slow split. The released checkpoint is branded GR00T-N1-2B and counts 2.2 billion parameters, 1.34 billion of them in Eagle-2. In the paper, that vision-language System 2 runs at 10 Hz on an L40. System 1 is a diffusion transformer that outputs actions at 120 Hz. Cross-embodiment comes from small embodiment-specific encoders and decoders around shared middle layers.

Where the data comes from

  • Teleoperation seeds it; an hour often yields minutes of useful data after resets talk†.
  • GR00T Mimic / DexMimicGen multiplies demos in simulation. DexMimicGen: 60 human source demos to about 21,000 generated; 90% real-world success on can sorting versus 0% from the human demos alone (arXiv 2410.24185). GR00T N1’s 3,000 demonstrations per RoboCasa task come from MimicGen, a different paper.
  • GR00T Dreams post-trains Cosmos on the robot's own data, prompts new verbs and scenes, filters the "dreams" with a reasoner, and recovers actions with an inverse-dynamics model or latent action codes.

HOVER trains a whole-body controller with an RL teacher that sees privileged state, then distills it into a student with randomized command masks so one policy can track root velocity, wrist poses, or joint targets. That controller is a separate paper from GR00T N1.

Benchmarks move fast

On the RoboLab-120 overall leaderboard, Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID scored 36.8% and GR00T N1.6 7.2% (NVIDIA's report quotes 39.7% vs 5.3% on the specific-instruction split) paper. By September 2026 a third-party 7B model, FLUX 3 Action, had already passed Cosmos 3 Nano. Treat any single model as a snapshot.

13Autonomous vehicles

AV stacks have moved from many specialized networks toward end-to-end models. Rare events have to be found in fleet data, varied, and generated.

Generate and vary

Cosmos for AV uses multi-control transfer conditioned on HD maps, boxes and LiDAR, plus video search over billions of clip embeddings to find cases like "a man in a wheelchair in a parking lot" talk†.

Scenario coverage

Foretellix's approach: measure what fleet data covered, find coverage holes, and fill them with augmented real drives or fully synthetic scenarios, then sensor-simulate and re-render with Cosmos.

Closed loop

OmniDreams and FlashDreams (chapter 11) let a policy drive inside a generated world. Score that loop by whether it ranks policies the way a trusted simulator does.

Safety architecture

NVIDIA Halos combines classical and end-to-end AI models, with platform, algorithmic, and ecosystem safety, across DGX, Omniverse and Cosmos, and DRIVE AGX. Halos OS sits on DriveOS, which NVIDIA describes as conforming with ISO 26262 ASIL D.

ISO 26262 and ISO 21448 (SOTIF) are the automotive functional-safety and intended-functionality standards. This manual does not cover certification.

14Smart spaces and agentic operations

A robot sees the world from the inside out. A warehouse or a city sees it from the outside in: cameras that know where the free pallet space is, which person is too close to a humanoid, and who crossed a restricted zone. NVIDIA's Metropolis stack builds vision agents for this view.

  1. Perceive: multi-camera 3D detection and tracking in a bird's-eye view.
  2. Specialist agents: a spatial VLM helped by segmentation and depth models; safety and metrics agents over a real-time analytics database.
  3. Orchestrate: an LLM routes each question to the right agents and tools, including USD search over the facility's digital twin.
  4. Evaluate: score both the final answer and the trajectory of tool calls.

For cities, the Video Search and Summarization blueprint wraps existing trackers with VLMs, LLMs and retrieval, deployed from a single edge GPU with tens of cameras up to servers with hundreds.

Design lesson

Break the problem into many small tools, so you can drop tools as vision-language models improve talk†.

15Industrial twins and logistics

Real-time engineering twins

A real-time engineering twin is interactive, physically accurate and in context. The trick from SIGGRAPH 2026 is to train an AI surrogate on trusted high-fidelity simulation, then explore thousands of designs a day with the surrogate instead of the solver talk†. The building blocks are Warp for custom GPU solvers, PhysicsNeMo for training physics-ML models, Omniverse libraries, and Kit CAE for heavy visualization.

Fleets and routing

NVIDIA cuOpt solves vehicle routing on GPUs. In a community demo, cuOpt ran as a microservice next to Isaac Sim, assigned jobs to a forklift fleet over a waypoint graph, and Isaac Sim drove each forklift through an Ackermann-steering Action Graph. Two forklifts finished six jobs in 1 min 5 s screenshot; physics mode was capped at 4× speed because faster made forklifts tip in curves talk†.

Measured with a CPU baseline

A greedy-insertion plus local-search solver on the same request structure found the optimal makespan for 2 forklifts and 6 jobs, and a 216 s makespan for 5 forklifts and 20 targets against a 164.7 s lower bound. Optimizing total cost instead of makespan left 2 forklifts idle, the same behavior the demo's author described measured.

Mega is the Omniverse blueprint for testing fleets of robot policies in a factory twin. The launch post names KION and Accenture.

17Evaluation and safety

Open-loop evaluation compares a model's outputs to logged data. Closed-loop evaluation lets the model's actions change what it sees next. Open-loop scores do not predict closed-loop behavior, which is why the field built Isaac Lab Arena, RoboArena, NuRec scenes and Cosmos-Dreams.

Benchmark or methodWhat it measures
Isaac Lab ArenaMassively parallel policy evaluation in simulated sites; one partner reported 100,000 parallel trials on its Arena-based benchmark talk†
RoboLab-120 / RoboArenaSimulated success across instruction styles / real-world pairwise ranking
PAI-BenchGeneration quality and control fidelity for physical AI world models
Ranking preservationDoes a cheaper simulator order policies like a trusted one?
Agent evaluationFinal answers and tool-call trajectories of operations agents

Judge the judge

Learned judges (a Reasoner deciding whether a robot succeeded) must themselves be scored. The protocol: generate rollouts with known ground truth (fall flags, distances, contact events), ask the judge, and report precision, recall and a confusion matrix per condition. Report which failures it misses (slow sinking versus a sudden fall), not only a score.

Safety layers named in the sources

Halos for AVs; Thor's safety island; deliberately reduced speed and torque in the lab, because a falling 100 lb robot is "a giant human nutcracker" talk†; and Cosmos 3's own warning that safety-critical control needs additional validation.

20An eight-week learning path

About eight focused hours a week. Each week ends with something that runs.

  1. OpenUSD. Layers, opinions, LIVERPS, references versus payloads. Build a layered robot asset and validate it. Chapter 4.
  2. Newton basics. Run the pendulum and URDF examples on CPU; write the five-class loop yourself. Chapter 6.
  3. Solvers and contact. Reproduce the solver bake-off; change contact stiffness and watch the settle height move.
  4. Policies in simulation. Run the ANYmal walking policy in Newton; map joint orders; understand observation vectors. Chapter 8.
  5. Robot software. Study the Isaac Sim drive graph and Nav2 bring-up; if you have an RTX machine, run it. Chapter 7.
  6. Data from pixels. Domain randomization, Gaussian splatting basics, why reconstructions degrade off-path. Chapters 9–10.
  7. World models. Read the Cosmos 3 report; write Reasoner prompts and a judge-scoring harness against ground truth. Chapter 11.
  8. Systems. Pick one application (humanoids, AVs, operations, twins) and write down its flywheel and its evaluation plan. Chapters 12–17.

22Glossary

Articulation
A tree of rigid bodies connected by joints: a robot. Its root prim carries ArticulationRootAPI.
AMCL
Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization; the Nav2 node that estimates the robot's pose on a map.
CUDA graph
A recorded sequence of GPU kernel launches replayed as one unit, removing per-step launch overhead.
DDS
The publish-subscribe middleware under ROS 2; nodes find each other within a domain ID.
Domain randomization
Varying appearance and physics during training so a model cannot overfit to one simulator.
Forward / inverse dynamics
Predicting the next observation from an action / recovering the action from observations.
Gaussian splatting
A scene made of 3D Gaussians fitted to images and rasterized in real time.
Hydroelastic contact
Contact computed over a pressure field across an area instead of at points.
LIVERPS
OpenUSD composition strength: Local, Inherits, Variants, rElocates, References, Payloads, Specializes.
MPM
Material Point Method; simulates granular and continuum materials with particles on a background grid.
Payload
A lazily loaded reference in OpenUSD.
PPO
Proximal Policy Optimization, the default RL algorithm in Isaac Lab's rsl_rl runner.
SDF
Signed distance field; the distance to a surface at every point, used for fast, tight collision.
SimReady
A content specification for OpenUSD assets that simulators and robots can use directly.
Sim-to-real gap
The difference between how a policy behaves in simulation and on hardware.
Software in the loop
Running the real robot software against a simulated robot.
Surrogate model
A fast learned approximation of an expensive simulator.
Teleoperation
A human controlling a robot remotely to record demonstrations.
VLA
Vision-language-action model: images and instructions in, motor actions out.
Warp
NVIDIA's Python framework for writing GPU (and CPU) kernels; Newton is built on it.
World foundation model
A large model that generates or reasons about how the visual world evolves.
XYZW
Newton's quaternion order (x, y, z, then w). MuJoCo and Isaac use WXYZ.
